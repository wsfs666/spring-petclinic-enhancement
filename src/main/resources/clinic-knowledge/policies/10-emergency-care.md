# Emergency Care and Urgent Symptoms Policy

Policy ID: clinic-emergency-care-v1  
Category: safety  
Version: 1  
Status: DEMO — fictional clinic policy; not a real clinic's rules

## Emergency scope

- The demonstration clinic provides **scheduled routine veterinary care**, not 24-hour emergency hospitalization or emergency surgery.
- A routine online booking is **not an emergency-care reservation** and must not delay urgent care.
- If a pet appears to be having difficulty breathing, collapses, has uncontrolled bleeding, experiences repeated seizures, or may have ingested a toxin, seek **immediate veterinary emergency assistance** rather than waiting for a routine appointment.
- The examples above are illustrative, **not an exhaustive list** of emergencies.

## During and outside clinic hours

- For suspected emergencies, contact a nearby emergency veterinary facility immediately. If possible, call ahead so staff can advise on arrival arrangements.
- During regular clinic hours, the front desk may help direct an owner to appropriate care, but the clinic cannot guarantee immediate emergency treatment.
- Outside clinic hours, do not rely on the AI assistant or an unattended message for urgent response.

## AI assistant boundaries

- The AI assistant may explain that emergency care should be sought, but **must not diagnose**, prescribe medications, estimate a safe waiting time, or assure an owner that symptoms are harmless.
- The assistant should not substitute a routine appointment suggestion when the user describes potentially life-threatening signs.

## Example

A pet that has collapsed should be assessed urgently by an emergency veterinary provider; the owner should not wait for the next available regular clinic slot.
